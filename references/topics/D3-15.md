# D3-15 — 3D performance visibility: LOD, HLOD and occlusion

When: Profiling identifies rendering cost and scene layout has distance or occlusion opportunities.

Native candidates: `MeshInstance3D`, `MultiMeshInstance3D`, `OccluderInstance3D`, `Occluder3D`, `GeometryInstance3D`.

## Approach

Consider import-generated mesh LOD, authored visibility-range representations, or baked/manual occluders according to measured bottleneck and level layout; profile and combine selectively.

Use engine LOD/visibility/culling mechanisms when they address the measured cost before writing custom distance culling.

## Pitfalls

- Occlusion baking ignores MultiMesh, particles and CSG by default.
- Moving complex baked occluders can be expensive.
- Over-aggressive simplification can hide visible objects.
- HLOD/occlusion setup may not pay off in open/simple scenes.

## Verify

- Measure representative camera paths with culling on/off and debug views.
- Confirm objects never disappear incorrectly.
- Profile target renderer/hardware.

## Version and limits

4.7 visibility and occlusion docs; import LOD behavior can depend on source format and import settings.

- [F3D25: Occlusion culling](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/occlusion_culling.html). Scope/limits: Benefits depend on view/layout; no benchmark across target hardware.

- [F3D26: Visibility ranges (HLOD)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html). Scope/limits: Thresholds are project/art specific and require visual/performance tuning.
